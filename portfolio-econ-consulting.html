Pay Equity · Employment Litigation · NYC Citywide Payroll · Aug 2026

Pay Equity in the New York City Municipal Workforce

Using the full FY2024 NYC citywide payroll — 217,402 full-time, full-year salaried employees, with sex imputed from first names via SSA birth records — this analysis measures the gender pay gap and separates the portion operating within jobs from the portion operating through which jobs men and women hold, the core allocation-versus- treatment question in pay equity litigation. Women earn 5.9% less in raw terms and 7.5% less after adjusting for tenure and agency; conditional on title, the within-job gap is a precise statistical null (-0.08%), while -7.42 points of the adjusted gap runs through job allocation, with women holding titles paying 9.1% less. Two methodological findings are reported alongside the estimates: a routine full-time screen that silently excluded a 74.5%-female workforce and doubled the apparent gap, and quantile standard errors that ignored clustering and inflated apparent significance by more than an order of magnitude.

Price Effects in Online Hotel Search

Estimates the causal effect of price on click-through and booking probability across 9.9M impressions and 399,344 search sessions from the Expedia hotel-search dataset, addressing unobserved quality and algorithmic ranking bias by exploiting sessions where display order is randomized. Eight specifications — pooled OLS, within-session fixed effects, IV/2SLS, and double/debiased ML — separate the causal price elasticity from confounded correlational estimates. The preferred within-session estimate implies a 10% price increase lowers click probability by ~0.28 percentage points (~6% of baseline) — a design directly applicable to demand estimation in merger simulation and overcharge quantification.

Algorithmic Pricing · Antitrust · Replication · Jun 2026

Simple Rules and Algorithmic Collusion

Replicates the restricted-action Q-learning pricing environment of Liu, Wang and Ghose (2026) — sellers choosing among Undercut, Match, and Above rather than a full price grid — against the unconstrained collusion benchmark of Calvano et al. (2020), a design relevant to tacit-coordination questions in algorithmic-pricing antitrust matters. Across 50 seeds the market locks at the monopoly price with zero dispersion (collusion index Δ = 1.000), though a robustness check the original paper reports at 100% replicates in only 28% of runs; because the Match rule sets price equal to a rival's by construction, the analysis argues this Δ = 1.00 is a weaker claim than emergent collusion under unconstrained competition. An extension replaces the perfect competitor-price signal with a noisy private draw to inform price-transparency regulation.

Causal Inference · Instrumental Variables · LMU Munich · Mar 2026

When Is TSLS Actually LATE? Evidence from Project STAR

Builds on Blandhol et al. (2022) to show that linear IV with covariates is only weakly causal when a "rich covariates" condition holds — that the linear projection of the instrument onto covariates matches its true conditional expectation — a specification question at the heart of any expert TSLS estimate. Applying the framework to Project STAR (N=11,599), standard TSLS fails the Ramsey RESET diagnostic in every grade cohort, and a Double/Debiased ML specification implies specification bias inflated the conventional estimate by up to 32.6% — larger than the selection bias the instrument was meant to correct.

-- Q14: Relational division
-- stores accepting ALL payment methods

SELECT pos.point_id,
       pos.name, pos.city
FROM POINT_OF_SALE pos
WHERE NOT EXISTS (
  SELECT 1
  FROM (
    SELECT DISTINCT payment_method
    FROM PAYMENT_METHOD
  ) allm
  WHERE NOT EXISTS (
    SELECT 1
    FROM PAYMENT_METHOD pm
    WHERE pm.point_id = pos.point_id
      AND pm.payment_method
        = allm.payment_method
  )
)
ORDER BY pos.point_id;
